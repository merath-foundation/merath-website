004.10 An overview

merath's Research Methods Workshop is where academic scholarship, strategic operations, and creative research are brought into a single working register — and where the instruments designed in that intersection are tested. The methods built here move between domains by design: catalogues that are arguments, manuals that are publications, archives that are gathering methods, performances that demonstrate how knowledge actually moves.

Artistic research, at merath, is not research about art. It is research conducted through the registers of practice — where form, method, and inquiry are designed together, where the work itself is the second argument the research makes, and where creative production is one of the inquiry's modes rather than its illustration. The Workshop develops the instruments that this kind of research needs to actually proceed.

The Workshop is necessary because the conditions require it. The region's past is partially obscured. Its future is contested. Its present is held by partial cultural infrastructure and a critical vocabulary often inherited from elsewhere or not yet shaped at all. The methods designed here are responses to those conditions, not impositions on them. They make possible the kinds of inquiry that scholarship alone, art alone, or consultancy alone cannot hold.

004.24 · The review
Where it stands now
Where the work stands now

The Workshop's designed methods are built and in use. The oral-history method, the Bilingual Catalogue Schema, and the Institutional Landscape are all operational, each already feeding the projects that need it, and all three merath Manuals are in circulation — m1 on financial and cultural economies, m2 on archaeological knowledge in fractured contexts, and m3 on private archives — turning method into portable form. The approach has reached the academy as well: merath presented the Workshop's methods as part of Columbia University's MESAAS visiting artists series.

The standing rituals hold the practice together: mR² keeps each member's research as shared institutional memory, and mAktoob carries twenty-three active threads toward publication. Beneath them the seven techniques — Listening, Writing, Mapping, Measuring, Translating, Performing, and Moving — are each at their own stage, carried by the projects that call on them. What the Workshop has shown is that a method built for one project can be designed to travel; the work now is making that travel dependable.

004.20 The current threads

In 2026, the Workshop's most public moments cluster around acoustic and editorial work.

merath has organised and put forward Fractured Orality: Methodologies of Listening in Libyan Studies — a panel at the Oral History Association annual meeting, gathering five papers across linguistic archives, archaeological oral history, transborder solidarity, the poly-vocal archive, and institutional memory. These are public moments. The standing work continues underneath them.

004.30 How the Workshop works

A collective does not have a method because one of its members has one. It has a method when what a single person worked out is held somewhere the others can reach — and can still reach once that person has moved on to something else.

Two standing sessions do that carrying. mR² takes one member's research into the shared practice. mAktoob takes a shared thread out to its public. Neither is administration around the work; they are where the work stops belonging to a person and starts belonging to the collective.

Both leave a record behind them — a transcript, a register, a checklist — which is the point rather than the paperwork. A practice held only in the room that invented it leaves when that room does.

004.40 mR²

mR² — mResearch mReflection — is the central one. A semi-regular internal session in which a member of merath presents their study of an institution, an initiative, or a method, and the team gathers to think with it. Sessions are recorded, transcribed, and kept as institutional memory. mR² is how a method enters the practice through one person's research and becomes shared rather than personal.

004.50 mAktoob

mAktoob — the story bank — holds every research thread merath is developing toward publication. Each thread is registered with form, format, phase, and supporting workflow. Stories pass through pitch sessions, where the team workshops how a piece reaches its public, and through editing reviews, where every submission moves through the integrity checklist. The voice alignment field is the editorial layer of the Workshop's collective voice — where house voice and house ethics are kept legible.

Pieces emerging from mAktoob are sometimes authored individually and sometimes under the merath byline. Recent collectively-authored work includes the OH Gallery piece Le correspondant ne répond plus à cette adresse and the merath text accompanying Balga / The Open Heel: The Ledger of the Unfixed at State of Fashion. mR² and mAktoob with their supporting sessions are the standing rituals. Without them, methods stay as documents. With them, methods stay alive.

004.60 Research methods designed

A method is designed when the existing ones do not fit. Each of these was built because the situation refused the standard approach — archives that are contested, dispersed or held elsewhere; economies that operate under sanction; testimony that carries a risk for the person giving it.

Three lines of that work. The first designs how material is held, on the argument that the shape of a catalogue is already a claim about what counts. The second studies the institutions and economies that decide what can be made at all, reading the historical and the contemporary against one another. The third hands the method on: the merath Manuals publish a way of working as an object to be handled rather than a description to be read.

What makes these methods rather than findings is that each is built to be carried to the next project, by someone who was not there for the first.

004.70 Archive and collection design

Archive design is research, not preparation for research. The architecture of a catalogue is the argument it makes about what counts. Two cases in active development illustrate the work.

The oral-history method was developed through the practice of gathering. The methodology — public exhibition as a safe, communal trigger for transgenerational testimony rather than an extractive interview model; bilingual interview metadata; submission forms inviting community contribution; consent and copyright workflows built for politically sensitive memory — was designed for and through the A Sun Dawned Over Algeria exhibition. The work belongs to 06 Libya-Algeria. The testimonies gathered through the exhibition became the substance of the project's publication, A Revolution Forged in Silver and Gold. The oral archive built around the exhibition is now merath's first sustained oral-history infrastructure, available to extend into other projects.

The Saharan Currency archive was developed through the project of the same name. The cataloguing system was built specifically to hold the trans-Saharan record: bilingual fields in Arabic and English, a Maghrebi OCR model trained on Ghadames manuscripts, and a relational schema that registers each currency by transaction, exchange rate, scribe, witness, route, and the goods it was used to acquire. The catalogue does not store the research; it enacts it. The schema is the analytic claim about what mattered in trans-Saharan trade.

004.80 Institutional and economic investigations

A second designed method is the strategic research the Workshop conducts on how cultural institutions operate and finance themselves — not only as the subjects of merath's projects, but as the conditions for operating as a collective in this region.

The Institutional Landscape maps cultural institutions across the region with their governance models, funding sources, political function, operational status, condition and proxy indicators, risk assessment, and stability analysis. The investigation draws on and feeds the Saharan Currency archive's historical study of exchange systems — the Mithqal standard, trans-Saharan trust networks, ledgers as instruments of authority — reading historical and contemporary cultural-economic models alongside one another. Currency databases and institutional models are studied together, because the conditions that make a cultural institution viable and the conditions that make a currency circulate are not finally separate problems.

This is the research that lets merath think strategically about its own operations: how to sustain a collective in a constrained funding landscape, how cultural production is financed under sanction and partial markets, which institutional models hold up under which conditions. The methods designed here shape merath's own institutional choices, and are extended outward as advisory work for others navigating similar terrain.

004.90 The merath Manuals

The Workshop releases its methods as published volumes in the merath Manuals series, each treating a different kind of research as itself the subject of a manual.

m1 Cultural Economies is a manual for financial and economic research. Designed as a hollowed nineteenth-century-style ledger that opens to reveal a tool tray on the left — a brass weight, a UV authenticity light, and a modern touchstone — alongside printed archival research on the right. The manual treats currency as a method: how value is measured across past and present, and what tools the researcher carries when measuring.

m2 is a manual for archaeological-knowledge research in fractured contexts. Framed as a field-manual glossary of verbs — blunt tools drawn from archaeology, geology, espionage, and scavenging — for working in places where archives are contested, decayed, or deliberately obscured. The manual offers a method for excavation in any field, by way of the archaeological one.

m3 A Framework for Private Archives is a manual for archive work in constrained contexts: how to organise and care for private archives when the political conditions for public ones are absent. It explains why cataloguing must come before digitisation, and reframes personal collections as living relational networks rather than chaotic boxes. It is a manual for the moment before an archive becomes public.

Each Manual takes a specific research method and turns it into a portable form. The methods do not stop being merath's; they become available to others.

004.100 Techniques of research

Beneath the designed methods, the Workshop is developing wider techniques of research — different ways of engaging with what the practice is trying to know. Each technique is at its own stage; each grows on its own bespoke terms. They are not equivalent areas of study. They are postures the Workshop takes toward the work.

These techniques are the directions the Workshop is currently extending. The list is not closed, and the techniques are not in competition. Each is being figured out at its own pace, by the projects that need it most.

004.110 Listening

Sound as evidence; testimony as method. The Workshop listens through merath's research on Libyan reel-to-reel tape archives — recovering raw phonic substance from the filters of nationalist transcription — through investigating Saharan dialectological traditions via our collaborators, and through the exhibition-gathered oral history archive of Libyan women in support of the Algerian Revolution. We share what we have learnt from this technique at the 2026 OHA panel Fractured Orality. Listening receives what writing cannot hold.

004.120 Writing

The technique that produces the form research takes when it leaves the studio. The Workshop writes through mAktoob's twenty-three active threads — moving from initial idea through pitch session, editing review, and the voice alignment check — into pieces published individually and under the merath byline. Genre research is the working library of forms. The editing & integrity checklist is the editorial layer. Pitching strategy is how the writing finds its publics.

004.130 Mapping

The Institutional Landscape — merath's analytic map of cultural institutions — registers governance, funding, political function, condition, and stability for each institution the practice might engage. The global directory of oral history archives is its companion document. Together they let merath decide which institutions to partner with, which to learn from, and which to carefully navigate.

004.140 Measuring

The Workshop measures through the Saharan Currency archive (the Mithqal standard and the trans-Saharan ledger of exchange), through the operational economics of cultural production under sanction, and through the Liminal/Overhead reframing of contemporary aerial surveillance as a form of forensic audit. Value is read as a form that carries political and historical content.

004.150 Translating

The technique runs through bilingual cataloguing across the practice — Arabic and English in projects 02 Saharan Currency, 06 Libya-Algeria, 09 Publishing Field and 10 Print Rahba — through the Darija / Fusha switching that surfaces in research on regional musical manuscripts, and through the ongoing question of what each language carries that the others cannot. Translation is treated as a methodological move, not as a service rendered after the work is done.

004.160 Performing

The technique tests what it means to encounter an audience in real time. The Workshop performs through live audits and real-time enactments. CEN wa SAD organises its public form around the assay-office logic — balances, stamps, and masking paper handled as instruments of public inquiry — staging trans-Saharan exchange as something the audience watches happen, not something they read about. m1's hollowed ledger holds the brass weight, the UV light, the touchstone; the publication is itself an object that asks to be handled. The A Sun Dawned Over Algeria installation reads as argument made spatial — the visitor encountered the gathered testimonies in the room. What an audience asks back, what falls apart on stage, what becomes legible only in the moment of demonstration: these feed back into the research. Performing is research that registers reception as a condition of its findings.

004.170 Moving

Migration and mobility are key realities of the region — and the forms through which much of its knowledge actually circulates. The Workshop treats movement as a method: research conducted on the move, where the route is the inquiry and the journey is one of the conditions for what gets learned. 01 Sarayān developed this technique through a mobile research programme and community video commissions across Senegal, Alexandria, Beirut and Tripoli — where pedagogy itself was treated as transmission across borders. 05 Archaeological Knowledge extends the technique into walking-as-method, opening Libyan archaeological sites to Libyan audiences with Dar Fergiani co-publishing the guidebooks. The Saharan Currency archive's relational schema registers routes between origin and destination across trans-Saharan trade, treating the trade route itself as a knowledge structure. The Liminal/Overhead piece moves through historical time — (1911-1956-1980)— tracing the genealogy of aerial surveillance and the evasion that grows alongside it. The route is the method; the map is the finding.

004.180 Activities

The Workshop is where the methods are made; the activities below are where they travel into the wider practice and outward into other people's work.

004.190 Inside merath

The Workshop's instruments and techniques have shaped every other merath project. The oral history method designed for 06 Libya-Algeria now informs the editorial framing of the Lamma special issue. The catalogue architecture first built for 02 Saharan Currency has been adapted into the bilingual schemas of projects 09 Publishing Field, 10 Print Rahba, and 06's interview metadata. The voice alignment field developed inside the editing & integrity checklist governs every piece moving toward publication across the practice. The Institutional Landscape underwrites strategic decisions across partnerships, grants, and engagements. The methods do not stay in the Workshop; they travel.

004.200 Editorial and conference work

merath organises and puts forward Fractured Orality: Methodologies of Listening in Libyan Studies at the Oral History Association annual meeting 2026. The Workshop is concurrently guest-editing the Lamma special issue Unwritten Archives; doing peer review for Lamma and other journals in the field; and has presented the Workshop's broader methodological approach at MESAAS — the Middle Eastern, South Asian, and African Studies department at Columbia University.

004.210 Training and workshops in archives

The Workshop delivers methodological training to other practitioners. Two areas have developed most substantially. Private collections: the Workshop runs sessions for collectors, family archives, and small institutions on how to organise, catalogue, and care for their materials when public archival support is absent and digitisation is premature. The training draws directly on m3. Cultural institutions: the Workshop offers methodological consulting on archive design, collection management, editorial integrity, and the assessment of institutional risk before engagement. The techniques developed inside merath's own projects — 02's catalogue architecture, 06's oral history method, the voice alignment editorial layer — are offered as portable practice. The Workshop also contributes its writing and editorial methods to the curriculum of 03 Al Manahjij: School of Schools.

004.220 Collectively-authored writing

Pieces under merath's collective byline emerge from mAktoob's pipeline.

Recent examples: Le correspondant ne répond plus à cette adresse (https://www.ohgallery.net/expositions-2025/le-correspondant-ne-repond-plus-a-cette-adresse) for OH Gallery's 2025 programme, and the text accompanying Balga / The Open Heel: The Ledger of the Unfixed at State of Fashion.

The Workshop's outward activities feed back into its inward work. New questions arrive through commissions and engagements. New methods get added because new conditions demand them.
